# Pi Pocket features

The full tour. For setup, see the [README](../README.md).

- **Durable runs.** Every model request, tool call, queued message, and subagent is a durable task in one SQLite file. Kill the server mid-run and start it again: the run continues. A tool call that was cut off reruns if it is safe to; otherwise the model is told it was interrupted.
- **Omarchy themes.** All of Omarchy's themes, from Tokyo Night and Gruvbox to Rosé Pine and the light ones, drawn from each theme's own `colors.toml`, with Omarchy's look: square tiles, 2px window borders, no shadows or blur, and JetBrains Mono throughout (bundled in `web/fonts/` under the SIL Open Font License). Menu → **Appearance** (or the **Theme** tile, the launcher, or `/theme gruvbox`) picks one; the new look grows as a circle from where you tapped. **Desktop** follows the Omarchy theme of the machine Pi Pocket runs on: change your desktop's theme and the app follows within seconds, optionally with your wallpaper in the gaps.
- **Tiled windows and motion.** On wide screens the sidebar and the session are Hyprland windows: gaps, an inactive border, and the active border on the one under the mouse. Motion uses Omarchy's Hyprland curves and timings: windows and sheets pop in at 87% and leave quickly, new messages slide in, and notices count down until they go. While Pi works, only the conversation moves; elsewhere a working session is marked but still. Appearance can turn tiling off, reduce motion (or follow the system's setting), change the text size, and show the reply speed under the message box.
- **The sidebar.** A rail and the session list beside it. The rail holds Pi (which folds the sidebar, as Ctrl/⌘+B does), the tools (Running now, with how many sessions work; the subagents board, with how many subagents work or wait, and a red mark when one waits for you or failed and nobody has dealt with it yet; People; Providers; Theme), and you. The list has Recent, Folders, and Archived tabs; sessions grouped by day or by folder, in groups that fold; and pinned sessions on top (per browser). Each session is one line: its folder's color, its title (bold with new chat or when marked unread, the accent when open), and on the right ⑂ and how many subagents work there (or failed with nobody dealing with it yet, in red), who else is there, a mark while Pi works or "!" while a call waits for you, a dot for new chat (or for a session this browser marked unread), and how long ago; its folder and model are in its tooltip. Hover a row to archive, pin, or mark it unread; on a touch screen, hold a finger on it (the row shows the press, and moving the finger scrolls instead) for a sheet that pins it, marks it unread or read, archives or unarchives it, or selects it. Marks are per browser, like pins, and opening the session clears its mark; the session's menu marks the open one unread and goes back to the list. Archiving says so in a notice that undoes it. Ctrl/⌘+click selects sessions, Ctrl/⌘+drag selects every row it passes (the list scrolls at its edges), and Shift+click selects a run; on a touch screen, after **Select**, taps select more rows or let them go. A bar under the list archives, unarchives, or pins them, and Esc (or back, after Select) lets go. Archiving does not move a session in the list, so one brought back returns to where it was. Drag its edge to resize the list, or fold the sidebar to the rail with numbered sessions, like Waybar's workspaces. On phones the drawer and the list with no session open have the same rail. On wide screens with no session open, the home screen shows a fastfetch-style summary and the sessions to jump back into.
- **Peek tiles.** Off until you turn them on: the menu's **Peeks** tile, Alt+P, `/peek`, the launcher, or Appearance switches them on or off in one step, and while they are on, the tiles button in the top bar does too (while off, the button shows on wide screens only when another session waits for you, with a dot). Then other sessions' live work shows beside the one you are in: one tile per session that is working, waiting for an approval, finished since this browser last had it open, or pinned, plus the one you just left. Each shows the session's last few steps (what was asked, what Pi said, its tool calls and how they went) and changes at most once a second, without motion. Click a tile to open its session; the one you leave takes its place, so the tiles stay put. Allow or deny a waiting call right on its tile, which shows the whole command and why the guard asks; a command that does not show whole offers Open instead of Allow, which goes to the conversation it waits in. The ✓ on a finished one marks it seen. On wide screens the tiles are a column that slides in where People and the Browser panel dock when neither is open, with **▲/▼ N need you** at its edge for waiting tiles out of view; narrower screens (and wide ones with a panel open) get a strip under the top bar that swipes sideways. Only the tiles on screen are live: once scrolling settles, the tab tells the server which, and the rest keep their last lines (their marks stay current from the session list); a tab in the background keeps none live, and with tiles off nothing is sent at all. Peeking is not being there: it does not show you in the session's people.
- **Launcher.** Ctrl/⌘+K, after Omarchy's Walker: one search box for sessions, actions, and themes, with fuzzy matching. Arrowing onto a theme shows it at once; Enter keeps it, Escape goes back. Start with `>` for actions, `@` for sessions, or `#` for themes.
- **Keyboard.** Alt+1…9 jumps to a session (hold Alt to see the numbers), Alt+↑/↓ goes to the previous or next one, Alt+N starts a new one, Alt+B opens or closes the browser, Alt+E the Files tile, Alt+V starts or stops dictation, and `?` lists every shortcut. On a computer, a session opens with the caret in its message box, ready to type; on a touch screen it waits for a tap, so the keyboard does not cover the conversation, and on a Mac a session opened with Alt+1…9 or Alt+↑/↓ leaves the box alone, so the next Alt shortcut still works (Option types characters in a box).
- **The conversation.** A column of turns, made for phones. Your messages are a filled row with a prompt mark (the accent for yours, the sender's color for someone else's), who sent each and when; Pi's replies are plain prose, streamed as they come. What Pi does between its words, its thinking and tool calls, folds into one line that says what it did and for how long ("Thought, searched, read 2 files, ran 1 command · 18s"), and opens into a timeline: a line per call with a mark for how it went and what it came to (lines added and removed, lines read, matches, tests passed, an exit code, "needs approval"), and tapped, its input and output, with the diff viewer's diffs for edits (line numbers, syntax colors, and the words that changed) and bash's live output. The newest turn's groups are open and earlier ones closed, until you open or close one. An approval sits at the end of its turn, and while Pi works one line says what it does (thinking, working, waiting for an approval, retrying) and for how long. While Pi works, **Steer** or **Queue** beside the model chip says whether a message steers the run or waits for it to end; queued messages can be withdrawn, and Stop (or Esc twice) stops the run.
- **Getting around on a phone, with one hand.** Back (the swipe, Android's back button, the browser's) goes back to where you were: it closes what opened last (a sheet, the menu's next sheet back to the menu, a file back to the Files tree, back to Changes when the file was opened from there, the Files tile or the Browser panel, the drawer) before it leaves the session, and leaving goes to the session before or to the list, scrolled and searched as you left it. A session opened straight away (a link, a notification) has the list under it, so back does not leave the app. What you use most is a thumb's reach from the bottom edge: the button beside the message box (or a swipe up from it) opens the places, a short sheet of big tiles (Files, Changes, Browser, Artifacts, Chat, Find, Branch, Peeks) with the session menu below them. On the conversation, swipe left for Files and right to go back; in Files, swipe right to go back. Each swipe shows a mark that lights when letting go will act. The top bar leads back to the list (counting other sessions that wait for an approval) and keeps only the buttons with something to say. Every control is a thumb's size on a touch screen (small ones, such as a code block's Copy or a reply's Pin, keep their look and take a wider tap); a tap anywhere on the message box, off its one line of text, still goes to the text; a quick second tap does not land on whatever just slid in under it; tables in replies scroll sideways instead of crushing their columns; a diff's header keeps the file's name on a line of its own on a phone; and the command a peek tile asks about opens its session when tapped. The Files tile and the browser slide in as screens of their own and slide away on back, a file slides in over the tree, and the browser's tools sit on their own row under the address. Drag a sheet down, or the drawer left, to close it. In an iPhone home screen app, which has no back gesture of its own, swipe in from the left edge to go back.
- **Forks.** Tap Pi's reply → More → **Fork from here**: a new session with everything up to that reply, which then goes its own way. Tap one of your messages to **edit it and send it again**, or send any message again, to the same model or another one (a retry). Each makes a new session that forks just before the message; the original stays as it is. A fork gets its own chat (with the pins of what it inherited), a link back to where it came from, and optionally a git worktree of its own.
- **A new context.** Menu → **New context** (or `/reset [note]`): Pi starts fresh and no longer sees the messages so far, while everyone still does. An optional handoff note carries over what matters.
- **Instructions for Pi.** Menu → **Instructions for Pi** (or `/instructions …`): ground rules Pi gets with every message in this session, such as "use pnpm, not npm". Everyone in the session can see them.
- **Memory.** Short lessons Pi keeps across sessions: how to build and test a project, a gotcha it hit, a convention, a preference someone stated. Pi proposes them with its `memory` tool as it learns (one or two sentences each, never secrets), and proposes fixing or forgetting one that turns out wrong; the owner approves. What Pi reads (a web page, a post, a comment, a file) could talk it into a "lesson" that would reach every later session, so nothing Pi saves, changes, or forgets counts until the owner says: **Approve** and **Discard** show right under Pi's memory call, with the words that wait and where they would apply (this project, or everywhere: every session and PiP), and in the sheet. Approve approves only what you saw: if Pi changed it since, the app says so and shows the new words. Lessons and project names are kept on one line, without invisible characters, so Pi reads only what the owner saw. A change or forgetting Pi proposes leaves the lesson as it was until then. Lessons you add yourself count at once. Each is for this project or for everywhere: a project's lessons reach every later session in the same repository, its worktrees included (or the same folder, outside a repository), and everywhere's reach every session and PiP. Menu → **Memory** (its hint says how many apply here and everywhere) lists them in those two groups, each with who saved it and a link to the session it came from (when you may see it), with Edit and Forget; type one in to add it by hand, for this project or everywhere, or use `/remember run the tests with make check` or **Remember…** on a message. A guest's lessons wait for the owner too, and a guest's change to one in force is a proposal they can withdraw. What waits shows at the top of the owner's sheet; the menu's hint says how many. Lessons waiting take no room from the approved ones, and a person (or Pi for them) may have 20 waiting at most. Only the owner, or whoever saved a lesson, changes or forgets it; viewers only read. A project keeps 40 lessons and everywhere 30; past that, forget one first. Changes show as lines in the chat. Turn it off in Extensions.
- **Plan mode.** The **Plan** chip by the message box (or `/plan`): Pi reads files and runs read-only commands, then proposes a plan; writing, editing, other commands, and subagents are blocked. **Approve plan** turns plan mode off and tells Pi to go ahead.
- **Scheduled messages.** `/schedule in 2h check the deploy`, `/schedule tomorrow 9am …`, or `/schedule every weekday 8:00 summarize CI`, or Menu → **Scheduled messages**: Pi gets the message at its time, in this device's time zone, even with nobody watching, and the people in the session get the usual "Pi finished" notification. Pi can schedule its own follow-ups with its `schedule` tool. Each schedule is a durable task: a restart while it waits keeps waiting, one after its time sends at once, and none is ever sent twice. A schedule is cancelled, with a line in the chat saying why, once its person can no longer steer the session or a spend limit is reached.
- **Done when.** `/until npm test`: after each of Pi's answers, the check runs in the session's folder; while it fails, Pi gets the end of its output and keeps going, for five checks at most. A bar above the message box shows how it stands. With Lancet Guard on, the check must be a command the guard allows outright. In plan mode the check waits until the plan is approved, and past a spend limit Pi stops going on. A check cut off by a server restart is not run again, as it may have done part of its work: Pi hears so, and its next answer is checked.
- **Mention files.** Type `@` in the message box to pick a file or folder from the session's folder: `@` alone lists its top, `@src/` a folder's own files, and anything else matches across the whole folder (`@srvapp` finds `src/server/app.ts`). Tab or Enter (or a tap) puts the path in, and a folder opens to its files. Pi gets the message as written, `@src/server/app.ts` and all, and reads what it names; or tap **Send them along** above the box to send the files' contents with the message, as Claude Code does (text files up to 100 KB each, images as images when the model takes them). The list is git's in a repository (so ignored files stay out), and a walk that skips hidden and dependency folders elsewhere; your browser fetches it once and matches as you type, so suggestions never wait on the network.
- **Shell commands.** `!git status` runs in the session's folder, and Pi sees the command and what it printed with your next message, as in Pi's terminal app; `!!git status` shows it only to the people here. While it runs, a row says so, with Stop (it is in Running now too). A restart while it runs does not run it again: its entry says it was cut off. With Lancet Guard on, a command from anyone but the owner must be one the guard allows outright. A command Pi sees counts as its person's message: Pi works for them from there, as for a steer. These are a person's own commands, not Pi's: plan mode does not hold them back. Stop one from its row, or from Running now on any device.
- **What you sent before.** ↑ in the message box goes back through what this browser sent (↓ comes forward, then back to your draft), and Ctrl+R searches it.
- **Dictation.** The mic beside Send, in the message box and in PiP's, dictates with the browser's own speech recognition, in the language the browser is set to. Words show in the box as they are heard and stay once the browser settles on them: at the caret while the box has the focus, after its text when it does not, with a space where they meet; words typed meanwhile stay too, and a phrase still being heard when you type or move the caret elsewhere finishes where it is, the next one going to the caret. It never sends by itself: tap the mic again (or Alt+V, which dictates into PiP's box while PiP is open, else into the session's) to stop, read it over, and send. Sending, leaving the session, closing PiP, or switching away from the tab stops it as well. What was heard is kept as a draft, like typing. While it listens the mic takes the accent, with a ring that breathes (still, with reduced motion), and the box says "Listening…". On a phone while Pi works, the mic takes Send's place until there is something to send. It needs a browser with the Web Speech API: Chrome, which sends the audio to Google's servers to recognise it, and Safari, on iOS too; where there is none (Firefox), the mic does not show. Browsers give a page the microphone only over https or on localhost, as Pi Pocket is served through the Cloudflare Tunnel, Tailscale, or on this device. A blocked microphone, silence, a missing microphone, or no network says so in a notice.
- **Long pastes.** Pasting more than 20 lines puts a placeholder such as `[Pasted text #1 · 240 lines]` in the box; the message goes out with the text in its place.
- **Files at a tap.** Inline code paths in Pi's replies (`src/app.ts`, `app.ts:42`), Markdown links to local paths (such as `[config](/etc/app/web.env)`), file mentions in messages, read/write/edit cards, and Changes open a viewer: text with line numbers and syntax colors (Markdown rendered too), images, and folders to browse, with **@ Mention** to put the file in the message box. While the Files tile is open, they open there instead of in a sheet. Someone invited to one session sees its folder only, and only the owner opens Pi's own folder and Pi Pocket's data.
- **Files tile.** Menu → **Files** (or Alt+E, `/files`, or the launcher; the folder button in the top bar while it is open) shows the session's folder beside the conversation on wide screens, where the Browser and People panels dock (one of the three at a time), and full screen on phones. **Files** is a tree of the folder that opens as you go (changed files are colored, folders holding changes are marked, and arrow keys move through it), a **Go to file** box that matches across the whole folder as the `@` menu does, and the file you pick, beside the tree when the tile is wide enough and in its place when not. **Changes** is the review below. Drag the tile's edge to resize it.
- **Find in session.** Ctrl/⌘+F, `/find`, or Menu → **Find in session**: every message, command, and note in the session, earlier history included, newest first; tap one to jump to it.
- **Stop with Esc.** Esc twice stops Pi while it works.
- **Session titles.** A session is named after its first message; a long first message, or one that runs a skill or prompt template (`/skill:name`, `/review …`), gets a short title written by the session's own model (its cost counts as the session's), unless someone renamed it first. A command's title is written from what it sends, not only its name. A reply that is no title, such as a model thinking aloud or one cut off at its length limit, leaves the first message's.
- **Prompt templates.** Pi's prompt templates (`~/.pi/agent/prompts/*.md`, the project's `.pi/prompts/*.md`, and the paths in Pi's settings) show up as slash commands: `/review src/auth.ts` sends the `review` template with its arguments filled in. Pi's skills do too, as `/skill:name what to do`: Pi gets the skill's instructions with the request.
- **Project trust.** A project's own skills (`.agents/skills`) load only in a project Pi trusts, as in Pi's terminal app. When a session's project has some and nobody has decided, the owner is asked above the message box: **Review** says what trusting it loads, here and in Pi's terminal app, and offers to trust the folder, the folder above it, or not; **Don't trust** answers at once, and **Not now** (×) puts the question off in that tab. `/trust` or Menu → **Project trust** changes the answer later. Answers are saved where Pi's terminal app keeps its own, so a project trusted there is trusted here too. Pi has the skills from its next message. A project's `.pi/skills` load until it is told "Don't trust", then stay out too (Pi's terminal app asks before loading them at all); the sheet lists both kinds and says what each answer does to them. Only the owner is asked.
- **Changes.** The Files tile's **Changes** tab (or the count of lines added and removed in the top bar, `/changes`, or Menu → **Changes**): every uncommitted change in the session's folder as one review, file by file. Each file has a header that stays in view while you scroll its lines, with what kind of change it is, its counts and a change bar, and a **Viewed** box that folds it; viewed files stay folded on this device until their diff changes again, and a bar counts how many you have seen. Diffs show side by side when there is room and unified when not (or as you pick, with long lines wrapped or not), with line numbers on both sides, syntax colors, and the words that changed within a line marked; a changed line is paired with its new version by likeness, not just position. The unchanged lines between changes are a tap away, a few at a time or all at once, read from the file as it is now (and only when it still matches the diff). Tap a line number to open the file there. A tree of the changed files (beside the diffs when wide, behind **Files** when narrow) jumps to one, a filter narrows them, and **π only** keeps the files Pi edited. Diffs load as they near the screen, a few at a time, and the review looks again a moment after Pi moves on. Keys: `j`/`k` the next or previous file, `n`/`p` the next or previous change, `v` viewed, `s` the layout, `w` wrapping. From a file's header, **↺** (tapped twice) puts it back as the last commit has it, or deletes it if it is new; Pi is told, and it waits while Pi works. A file with a merge conflict, or a rename, is left to git. Outside a repository it lists Pi's edits alone. Someone invited to one session sees the changes in its folder only.
- **Running now.** Menu → **Running now** (also on the sidebar's rail): everything at work in the sessions you can see, from Pi Durable's task graph: answers, tool calls, subagents, compactions, scheduled messages, and calls waiting for approval, with Stop and Cancel.
- **Watch a pull request.** Menu → **Watch pull request**, `/watch` (or `/watch 123`, `/watch <PR address>`), or Pi's `watch_pr` tool, which it can start after opening a PR (Pi only for its folder's repository, by number; a person for any): until the PR is merged or closed, Pi Pocket looks at it on GitHub every few minutes with the `gh` command signed in on the server, and tells Pi in the session what needs doing, as a message sent through the watch for whoever set it up: checks that failed on its last commit (once all of them finished, with the end of each GitHub Actions log), conflicts with its base branch, and new comments and reviews. Only comments from the repository's owner, and from organization members and collaborators whom GitHub says can write to it, count, and bots' reviews (a member may only read, a collaborator only triage: `gh` asks GitHub, at most every ten minutes for each person, and GitHub tells only an account that can push there, so on a PR where yours cannot, such as a fork's PR to someone else's repository, only the owner's count, and the watch says so); your own account's (which Pi posts as) only when they mention @pi in their own words (not in a quote or code), so Pi never answers itself. Comments keep their lines (a suggested change is code), 30 at most in a message (read newest first, so a burst never hides the new ones); each is told once, and what does not fit in one message comes in the next; nothing goes while Pi is busy there or, with take turns on, while someone else drives (the bar says it waits, and nothing counts as failed); and a watch sends 10 messages at most. While Triggers is off it asks GitHub nothing. A bar above the message box shows the PR, its checks, and how many messages went, with **Stop**. It ends, with a line in the chat and a notification, when the PR is merged or closed, when there is more to tell than it may send, after two weeks with nothing new, or after an hour of looks that failed (the bar says why). Logs reach Pi fenced as data, which nothing in them can end early, and titles, check names, and branches come on one line, quoted, as data too. It needs `gh` installed and signed in (`gh auth login`) on the server, on GitHub Enterprise hosts too: a host other than github.com is used only when `gh` is signed in there (`gh auth login --hostname <host>`; a token only in `GH_ENTERPRISE_TOKEN` does not count), for Pi, PiP, and people alike, and Pi names a PR by address only on github.com or its folder's own host.
- **Watch a repository.** Tell PiP in plain words, such as "watch acme/api: when CI fails on main, fix it and tell me" or "when I open an issue labelled pi in acme/web, work on it": it sets up a watch that looks at the repository on GitHub every few minutes with `gh` (no public address needed, unlike a webhook), and what is new there (issues and pull requests opened, CI failing on a branch, releases, someone writing @pi, as a word of its own, in a comment or an issue) comes to PiP, or to a session you name, with your instructions; PiP then acts on it: it starts a session in the repository (its checkout here, or a clone it makes in the data folder's `repos/`), sends it the task, follows it, and tells you what came of it. A watch can be narrowed to a label, to issues assigned to you, or to a branch's CI (mentions of @pi are not narrowed). CI is the branch's own runs: pushed to it, scheduled, started by hand or through the API, or started by another workflow, in the repository itself, never a pull request's or an issue's (a renamed repository keeps working). Only repositories `gh`'s account can push to can be watched or worked on, and issues and pull requests count only from the repository's owner, and organization members and collaborators whom GitHub says can write to it (and bots' pull requests), unless you turn on **From anyone** on the watch in Menu → Webhooks and watches (PiP cannot); @pi always only from them. PiP works in a checkout of a session you started, or in a clone it makes, which appears only once complete (a clone a restart cut off leaves a `~part-…` folder, removed at the next clone of that repository); a different folder already where the clone goes is reported, never overwritten. While Triggers is off, watches wait and ask GitHub nothing; while PiP is off, a watch to PiP holds what it found, and its note says so. What came from GitHub is fenced as data, each thing is told once, ten in a message and twelve messages an hour at most (the rest come next), and a look that fails is tried again, less often each time, with the reason shown. Menu → **Webhooks and watches** lists the watches, to remove one; or ask PiP ("what are you watching?", "stop watching acme/api"). PiP can also start a PR watch on a session ("see acme/api#12 through"), and Pi's own `watch_pr` takes a PR in another repository it can push to by its address or `owner/name#12`, telling Pi to work in a checkout of it. Part of the **Triggers** extension.
- **Webhooks.** Menu → **Webhooks and watches**: an address that other services (GitHub, a deploy, a monitor) post to, each post becoming a message to Pi in a session, or to PiP, as the work of whoever made the hook: their instructions first ("When a deploy fails, find out why and fix it"), then what was posted, trimmed of noise such as API addresses, and fenced as data. PiP gets them as folded cards and decides what to do: start a session, message one, or tell you. A GitHub hook is signed: GitHub gets the address and, separately, a secret (its Payload URL and Secret), and a post gets in only with GitHub's signature made with it, so whoever sees the address (the repository's admins do, in its settings) cannot post as the hook. Unless a GitHub hook is made **From anyone**, what people outside the repository wrote does not reach Pi (the repository's people are its owner, and the organization members and collaborators whom GitHub says can write to it, asked through `gh`, which must be signed in to the host and able to push there, or else only the owner counts; bots count on pull requests and what is said on them, never on issues): a post an outsider started (their issue, comment, pull request, review, or discussion) is dropped with a 202 that says so; in a post the repository's people started, the body of anything an outsider wrote in it (the issue under a member's comment, a review thread's comments) is left out, its title kept; a workflow run goes only when a branch of the repository itself started it (a push, a schedule, a manual or API dispatch, or another workflow), so one a pull request (a fork's too), an issue, or a comment started is dropped, and so is one whose head repository GitHub does not show; workflow jobs, check suites, and check runs do not say where their run came from, so they are dropped too, as are a commit status on no branch of the repository (a fork's commit) and a security advisory someone reported; and pushes, releases, and other posts that name no author go as they came. A hook for another service has the secret in its address instead: anyone who has the address can message Pi as the hook's person. **New secret** shuts out the old one, for an address or a secret that got out. Anything else gets 404. A post GitHub sends again goes to Pi once, GitHub's ping gets an answer and is nothing for Pi, a hook takes 30 posts an hour of up to 5 MB each (the server reads eight at a time, two to one hook, each within 15 seconds; past that, 503 with Retry-After), a hook to PiP answers 503 while PiP is turned off, and a post that cannot go now (someone else drives, a spend limit) gets a 409 that says no more. Services must be able to reach the server: through a tunnel, a public address works for GitHub. People who can steer make hooks for the sessions they can reach; each sees their own, the owner every one. The watch and webhooks are the **Triggers** extension: turned off, watches wait and hooks answer 503.
- **Spend and limits.** Menu → **Spend**: what Pi spent per person (the work goes to whoever asked for it) and per session, subagents included. The owner can limit a session or a person: past the limit Pi takes no new messages there, scheduled messages and goals stop, a run that crosses it stops, and subagents' reports wait instead of starting Pi again: the subagents bar says why (the owner gets **Raise it**), and they go to Pi together once the limit is raised, those that were in the queue when the run stopped included. The status line shows a session's spend and limit. PiP's row shows its limit too, and PiP's notices (such as a stop at its limit) show in its panel. Spend per person counts from the first start of a version with this feature; what was spent before shows for its sessions only.
- **A session's folder.** **New** (or Alt+N) opens a folder picker: browse from the default folder (hidden folders on request), pick a recent one, or type a path. **New folder** makes a folder inside the one you are in and opens it, ready to use; a typed path that is not there yet offers **Create it**, and the button becomes **Create and use**, which makes it and starts the session in one tap. Menu → **Working directory** (or `/cwd`) moves a session with the same picker. Someone invited to one session cannot browse or make folders.
- **Branch.** The top bar shows the git branch the session's folder has checked out, under the session's title beside the folder, and follows it when Pi or anyone else switches (in a terminal too, within seconds). Tap it (or Menu → **Branch**, the launcher, `/branch`, or the branch in Changes, which stays in sight however long the folder's path) for the branch picker, down from it: the folder's branches with the current one first, then the newest, each with its last commit and how far it is ahead of or behind its remote branch, and the remote branches it has no local branch for. Type to search; a name that is no branch yet makes one from what is checked out (spaces become dashes), and a remote branch becomes a local one that follows it. Uncommitted changes come along, and when git would overwrite them it says so and nothing changes. A branch checked out in another worktree is listed but switched to only there. Switching changes the files under Pi, so as with moving the session to another folder it waits while Pi works and, while taking turns, is the driver's; Pi is told. Viewers see the branch; someone invited to one session can look but not switch.
- **Worktrees.** A new session (the New session sheet) or a fork (the message sheet) can work in a git worktree of its own: a branch, apart from the folder it came from, which starts with that folder's files as they are. Menu → **Worktree** removes it (not while Pi works in it); its branch stays.
- **Continue a Pi session.** A session of Pi in the terminal can carry on here. The owner picks it from **Continue a Pi session** in the New session sheet (or the launcher): Pi's sessions on this computer by folder, newest first, with a search through their titles, folders, and words, and for one its folder, model, and last messages. **Continue in Pocket** makes a new session in its folder, with its model when that one is signed in here, and its whole conversation to read. Pi gets the context Pi itself builds from the file: what a compaction summarized, and Pi's context edits, stay as Pi had them. Pi's system prompt and extensions' state stay behind, and nothing Pi did runs again: a tool call that never finished gets a result saying so. The session's file is only read. The list marks sessions already continued here, and says when Pi went on in the terminal since.
- **Export.** Menu → **Export as Markdown** (or `/export`): the whole history as a Markdown file, with tool calls folded into `<details>` blocks.
- **Any provider or model.** Uses Pi's own model runtime and sign-ins (`~/.pi/agent/auth.json`): pick a model and thinking level per session from the menu that opens up from the model chip in the message box (arrows and Enter pick, with a search box when there are many models), and sign in to providers (API key or subscription) from the Providers sheet. New sessions start with the owner's default model and thinking level: **Make default** under the thinking levels makes the session's the default, and Menu → **Default model** (or the launcher) picks one without changing a session; without one, a new session starts with the last model anyone picked, then Pi's own default. A model is always one a person chose: a default that is not signed in now stays the default (the chip shows it, and the first message says why it fails) rather than give way to another model, and with no model chosen anywhere a new session starts with none until someone picks one. The model menu shows everyone what new sessions start with. A subscription sign-in that its provider will no longer renew (OpenAI's refusing a refresh with `invalid_grant`, say) is found when the owner opens the Providers sheet, when a subagent on another model or PiP is about to use one of its models, or when a run fails on it: the sheet marks the provider **needs sign-in** with what the provider said, the model menu marks its models, the owner (alone) gets a notice, and subagents on other models and PiP use none of its models until someone signs in again. A session on one of its models, and a subagent on that session's model, are not checked first: they try it, and their runs fail where you see them. **Models for subagents**, at the top of the owner's Providers sheet, lists every signed-in model by provider, with a box to find one when there are many: each one checked is a model Pi may start subagents on besides their session's own, none by default (see Background subagents; `subagentModels` in `~/.pi-pocket/config.json`). A tap changes that one model alone, so quick taps, and two tabs at once, keep every choice. An allowed model that is not signed in now stays listed, marked, so it can be taken off.
- **Multiplayer.** Any number of browsers can watch and steer the same session live. The status line under the message box shows how many are here, how full the context is (after a compaction, an estimate marked ~ until the next answer measures it), the cache hit rate, the cost, and whether Lancet Guard is on. **Reply speed** in Appearance (or the launcher) adds how fast the last answer came: its tokens per second and how long its first token took (a response delivered in one buffered flush has no speed, only that wait). It is off until you turn it on, in each browser. On a narrow screen the line keeps to one row where it can. Where it does not fit, it gives up these in turn, only as far as it must: the speed's long words ("42 tok/s · 1.2s"), "1 here" while you are alone, the word "guard" (the shield stays), the word "context" (the meter stays), the cache, and the wait for the first token. "Sign in another device" makes a one-time invite link, a QR code, and a short code to type on the other device's sign-in screen. The owner can tick **Sign in as me (owner)** to sign their own other device in as the owner, with the owner's notifications, instead of as someone new. Messages show who sent them.
- **Working together.** The top bar shows who else is in the session (an avatar with a mark that greys when their tab is in the background, and how many more), and "Alex is writing to Pi…" warns before two people steer at once. The **People** panel opens beside the conversation on wide screens, where it takes the Browser panel's place, and as a sheet on phones. It has:
    - **Chat** for the people in the session, which Pi does not see. Type `@` to mention someone (they get a notice wherever they are), tap **Discuss** under a reply of Pi's to quote it, and tap a chat message to **Send to Pi**, pin, or copy it. Changes show up here as activity lines ("Alex stopped the run", "Tanner switched the model to …", "Alex allowed the bash call: …"), and as a notice to everyone else in the session.
    - **Pinned** replies and chat messages.
    - **Notes**: one shared page per session. Saving over someone else's newer save asks first.
    - Under Pi's answers: emoji reactions, **Discuss**, and **Pin**. Queued messages show who queued them. Approved or denied tool calls show who decided.
- **Take turns.** Menu → **Take turns**: one person drives and only they send to Pi or change its settings. Others tap **Ask to drive**, the driver hands over, and anyone can take the wheel when the driver has left (the owner always can). Anyone who can steer can still stop a run.
- **Roles and invites.** An invite can let someone **steer** or make them **view only** (they read, react, and chat, but cannot send to Pi, approve, or upload). It can cover **every session** or **only this session**, and lasts 15 minutes, an hour, a day, or a week (**Expires after**); either way it works once, and a restart keeps it. Changing what the sheet's invite grants, or how long it lasts, ends the one it showed; **New invite** makes another and leaves the one before working. Only the owner can make an owner invite (**Sign in as me (owner)**): it makes no one new, but signs the device in with the owner's token, so it has full control, works once, and always expires in 15 minutes; rotating the owner token (`--rotate-token`) signs out every device signed in that way, and ends owner invites not yet used. Menu → **People** lists everyone, who is online, and when the others were last here. The owner can change what each person may do or remove them. The session list shows who is in each session, and a dot marks new chat messages.
- **Notifications.** Menu → **Notifications** turns on push notifications for a device: Pi finished, Pi needs approval, someone @mentioned you, or a chat message in a session you take part in. You only get them while you are not looking at that session. An approval can be denied from the notification, and allowed there when the whole call fits on one short line. The home-screen icon counts the sessions that need you. They need an https address: the Cloudflare Tunnel, or `tailscale serve` in front of **This device only** (`--access local`). On iPhone, add Pi Pocket to the home screen first. The server signs pushes with its own keys in `~/.pi-pocket/push.json`.
- **Built-in browser.** Menu → **Browser** (or Alt+B, `/browser localhost:5173`, or the globe in the top bar while a page is open; on a phone, a dot on the places button by the message box says so) opens a real Chromium page that runs on the server: beside the conversation on wide screens, full screen on phones. Type an address (`localhost:5173`, `example.com`), or (the owner) tap one of the web servers running on the machine; tap, drag to scroll, and type into the page (the keyboard button sends text from a phone). The size button switches between fitting the panel, desktop, phone, and tablet sizes, and the console button shows the page's logs and errors, with a count of errors in red. Pi's `browser` tool uses the same page: it opens addresses (dev servers, HTML files on the machine, any site), reads a snapshot of the page's text and controls, clicks, types, takes screenshots it can see, reads the console, and runs JavaScript in the page, while everyone in the session watches. Each session has one page, in a browser context of its own; links that open a new tab open there instead, and dialogs are accepted. Anyone in the session can watch; using the page takes steering rights (and the wheel, while take turns is on), the addresses people open show in the chat, and only the owner opens files from the address bar. The browser reaches what the server reaches, `localhost` and its network included, and Lancet Guard does not check it (see SECURITY.md). A restart closes the pages, and each opens again at its last address and size. In plan mode Pi may look at pages but not click, type, or run scripts in them. It needs Chromium, Chrome, Brave, or Edge on the server (or `PI_POCKET_BROWSER`); the owner can turn it off in Extensions, which closes the panel too.
- **Artifacts.** The `artifact` tool publishes versioned HTML pages, Markdown documents (shown in the app's theme), or SVG images that open in a sandboxed viewer (or their own tab). Artifacts cannot reach the app or its cookies.
- **Background subagents.** The `subagent` tool spawns named subagents that work in their own conversations while you keep talking. Open one to watch it or talk to it. Its answer goes back to the main agent at its next pause (after a tool call; when it is idle, after a moment, so answers that finish together go together); answers that arrive meanwhile wait together and go as one message, shown as a card each, so many subagents cost the main agent one extra turn at most, and wait in its queue as one row: one that arrives while that row waits joins it. A subagent that fails says why, and one that is stopped says so, to the main agent too, so it does not wait for an answer; a message sent to it while it worked, which its failed run never took, is withdrawn, and the main agent is told to send it again. A subagent runs on its session's model, thinking as hard as the session or less (a level its model lacks becomes the nearest lower one it has, and the main agent is told), with an optional tool list. Pi picks no model: until the owner allows other models for subagents (**Models for subagents** in the Providers sheet), the subagent tool offers none and refuses one Pi names; then Pi may use only those, and one whose sign-in expired is refused too, with word to tell you rather than pick another. A subagent on a model the owner no longer allows gets no more work, and one thinking harder than its session (started before this, or raised by hand) is lowered to the session's level as work is sent. One whose model or provider failed (an expired sign-in, a model that is gone or refused, a quota or rate limit) says so with its model, on its card and in a notice, and the main agent is told to stop and tell you, never to start it again on another model.
- **PiP.** The owner's assistant, "Pi in Pocket", over every screen. It stays out of the way until called: Alt+A, the rail's orb, the PiP tile in the places, the launcher, or `/pip` (`/pip what needs me?` asks at once); a small mark shows in the corner only while it works or has something new. It opens as a window anchored to the bottom right corner on wide screens (on a short screen it is shorter, never over a session's top bar; on a screen narrower than about 1600 px it covers the right end of a session's message box until it closes) and a sheet from the bottom on phones, over whatever is open, which stays as it was; Esc, back, or a swipe down closes it. PiP looks after the other sessions: it lists and reads them, starts sessions (in a folder, a worktree, with a model) and messages them, stops and archives them, schedules messages there, watches your GitHub repositories and pull requests and acts on what happens there (see Watch a repository), starts sessions in a repository by name (its checkout, or a clone), and follows runs until they end, when a report comes back to it and it says in a line what came of it: pushed as a notification when you are not looking (instead of the session's own "Pi finished", which is held back only while PiP is on, not held back by a spend limit, and following that run, and for a minute after its report), which opens PiP over whatever the app shows. It is dependable before fast: it acts on a session (messages, stops, archives, follows its run, schedules there, or watches its PR) only after it looked at it in the last 15 minutes (read it, started it, or had a report from it), its tools say how a session stands after they act, and it says what it checked and what it did not; tasks it sends that touch code ask for the checks and tests. It never picks a model: which one is yours to say, as below. What sessions say reaches PiP fenced as data, never as instructions, and what sessions, guests, or GitHub named (titles, waiting calls, scheduled messages, PR titles) comes on one line, quoted, as data too. It makes at most 60 messages, follows, schedules, and watches (its reminders to itself included; a schedule it could not make does not count), and starts at most 20 sessions, an hour (counted since the server started). It can deny a call waiting for approval, never allow one. When one of its own calls waits for approval, the panel says so, and **Review** opens its conversation, where it can be allowed. What it sends shows as sent through PiP for you; it reads files but changes none and runs no commands, handing hands-on work to sessions. Its replies link the sessions they name; **Open as a conversation** shows every step, and **Start fresh** gives it a new context. Its context stays small, as PiP needs no long memory of its own: the panel's header shows how full it is, as the status line does a session's; **Compact** there (or `/compact`, or `/compact` with what to keep in mind, typed to PiP) turns its older messages into a short summary, the newest few kept as they are; and it compacts itself once idle above 60k tokens of context (the owner picks 40k, 60k, 100k, or only as sessions do, in Extensions under PiP; `pipCompactAt` in `~/.pi-pocket/config.json`), then waits for 8k tokens more before it does again. A session's thresholds (Pi's own settings) stay as a backstop during a long run. PiP's summary is its own: written from its conversation with what sessions said, webhooks' posts, GitHub's events, and what files and scripts gave back left out, so none of their words come back as PiP's notes; it keeps its person's preferences, what is still open, and what it follows and watches, which live on the server and carry on as they were. A summary that cannot be written leaves the context as it was: Compact's notice says why, and after one of PiP's own that failed it waits 30 minutes, or 8k tokens more, before it tries again. A summary costs a request, counted in PiP's spend. It is a conversation of its own, kept out of the session list and out of everyone else's reach (nobody else can open it or write to it), and durable like the rest: follows outlive a restart. Its cost shows in its person's Spend sheet, and its follows in Running now. Turn it off in Extensions: it stops what it was doing (a compaction too), takes no messages, its own conversation's box included, and meanwhile its follows' reports and its own scheduled messages wait, webhooks to it answer 503, and repository watches to it hold what they found, all going on once it is back.
    - **It never picks a model.** It starts a session on one, or names one in a task, only when you named that model yourself, and not one whose sign-in expired:
        - in your own words to it, in its current context, with the whole name in one message. A short name of two characters, like "K3", counts only with its family's name in the same message, "Kiwi's K3": alone, it reads as an ordinary code.
        - or in the instructions of the webhooks and watches to it that you set up. A watch PiP set up, a webhook's post, a watch's event, or what a session wrote does not count.
    - **When the model you named is at two providers,** you say which one pays. Part of a provider's name picks nothing, after "on" or anywhere else, and PiP asks you which one instead: "on copilot" does not pick GitHub Copilot, and "build the server with Go" does not pick a provider called "Acme Go". Name it:
        - by its whole name or id, right after "on", "via", "through", "with", "using", or "from", in one message: "on GitHub Copilot", "via github-copilot".
        - or, when PiP asks which one, by answering with just its whole name or id as your next message: "GitHub Copilot".
    - **Some words name a model more loosely than you may mean:**
        - a short name of three characters or more, without its family's name: "an OpenAPI v3.1 spec" names a signed-in "Acme V3.1".
        - everyday words that are a model's name without its first word: "deep research" names "X9 Deep Research", and "the 2.5 flash drive" names "Nova 2.5 Flash".
        - a longer name Pi has no model for: it names the model it starts with. "zeta-5-ultra" names zeta-5, while "zeta-5-nano", a model Pi knows, does not.
- **Returning from a subagent.** A subagent's conversation has **Back to main session** above the message box, for viewers too, so the way back stays in sight however long the conversation is. The top bar says which session it is a subagent of.
- **Subagents bar.** Above the message box while a subagent works or has a report on its way: a dot per subagent, how many work and how many are done, and what the newest one does now. Tap it (or the count in the top bar while one works) for a row each: what it was asked, what it does this moment (the call it runs, or what it writes), for how long, whether its report is on its way, **Stop**, and a tap to open it; a call waiting for approval says so, and a failed one says why, and "dismissed" once someone dealt with it (see Subagents board). Done, stopped, and failed ones are counted apart, failed ones first. Once none works it stays until you put it away (×), and comes back, counting only the new ones, when one starts again. Viewers see it too, without Stop.
- **Subagents board.** The rail's ⑂ shows every subagent in every session in the conversation's place: a square each, in a row per session, those that need you first. A bar says how many wait for an approval, failed, work, were stopped, or are done, and filters by one; a search finds a subagent by its name, what it was asked, what it does now, or why it failed. **Needs you** lists those waiting for an approval and those that failed with nobody dealing with it yet. The one selected shows its session, how long it has worked (or when it ended), what it was asked and does now, and its error, with **Open** (**Review the approval** while it waits), **Stop**, and, for a failure, **Dismiss** (off while it is asked; when the subagent failed again since, a notice says so and the newer failure stays). A failure is dealt with once someone who can steer dismisses it, or opens the subagent from the board, its row in the subagents bar, its report or the tool call that started it, or the session menu: it stays on the board as failed ("Failed · dismissed", without the "!"), and leaves Needs you, the rail's red mark, and the session's red ⑂, for everyone, after a reload or a restart too. A message someone sends it, like later work from Pi, settles it by what it comes to, after a restart too: answered, the subagent is done; failing again, it needs someone again. The session's activity says who dealt with a failure, whichever way. Hover a square to see what it does in the line under them; the arrows move the focus between squares and Enter opens the one focused; Esc, back, or × closes it, and on a phone a swipe right does too. Archived sessions show only while a subagent works there. A session keeps its 24 newest finished squares (done, stopped, or failed and dealt with); older ones are counted, as **+N** after its squares and in the bar's numbers. On a phone it is one column, Needs you first, with bigger squares and a bar at the bottom for the one selected.
- **The queue and the bars above the message box.** Messages waiting for Pi show above the message box, with how many wait whenever not all of them show. With the bars there (the subagents bar, plan mode, a goal, a PR watch, a trust question, who drives), they take less than half of the room they share with the conversation when the bars fit in it, and the conversation keeps the rest, staying where you were reading. The bars always show whole; the queue and the subagents' list take what the bars leave, and scroll. Unfolding the subagents bar gives the list the room, one whole row at least: the queue shows fewer rows, or folds to how many wait. With several bars on a short screen, the plan, goal, watch, and trust bars keep one line each, their words cut short and their buttons whole. When even that does not fit (a phone on its side with every bar up), the stack scrolls, and the conversation keeps a quarter of the room, or a few lines, whichever is less.
- **Live editing.** No build step. Edit a file in `web/` and every open browser reloads. Edit a file in `src/server/extensions/` and it is reinstalled into the running server (a running tool call finishes on the old code). For core server changes, use **Restart server** in the menu: running work resumes after the restart.
- **Lancet Guard.** Off by default: the owner turns it on in Menu → **Extensions**. If `specpi-lancet-guard` is installed in Pi and turned on there too, its rules and classifier check every bash, write, and edit call. A "risky" or "unsure" verdict shows Allow/Deny buttons to everyone in the session; the answer survives restarts.
- **Codemode.** On by default, like `"+codemode"` in Pi's `defaultTools`. The `codemode` tool runs JavaScript the agent writes in a QuickJS sandbox with no Node, files, network, or timers; its only capability is calling the agent's other tools as `tools.<name>(args)`. Use it to batch independent calls, chain them, or filter large output before it reaches the model. Each call a script makes goes through the same checks and hooks as a direct call, so Lancet Guard judges it and can ask for approval. `store()` and `load()` keep values per conversation. Its card shows the script, each call it made, and the output.
- **Share to Pi.** With Pi Pocket on the home screen (Android), share a link, some text, or a screenshot from any app to Pi Pocket: pick a session or a new one, and it goes into the message box with the files attached, ready to send.
- **Approvals need someone else.** An owner setting under Lancet Guard in Menu → **Extensions**: a guest cannot allow a risky call that their own message led to. Someone else has to; the owner always can.
- **Docs for agents.** Pi's system prompt says where Pi Pocket's documentation is ([index.md](index.md)), to read when someone asks about Pi Pocket itself: changing how Pi works without code, writing extensions (with tested examples), and changing Pi Pocket's code while it runs, checked first on a throwaway copy (`node test/serve.ts`).
- **Drop-in extensions.** Put your own extension module (a `.ts` file that default-exports `(host) => Extension`) in `~/.pi-pocket/extensions/`. It shows in the Extensions sheet, stays off until the owner turns it on, can import Pi Durable and pi-ai like the built-in modules, and reloads when edited. One that would take the name of a built-in extension or tool, or of another drop-in's, is not loaded, and the sheet says why: installing it would replace that one.
- **Extensions sheet.** Menu → **Extensions** (or tap "guard" in the status line) lists Pi Pocket's extensions with their tools and any load error. The owner can turn Artifacts, Browser, Subagents, Scheduled messages, Done when, Plan mode, Codemode, Memory, PiP, Triggers, Lancet Guard, or a drop-in off and on, or reload one; the change applies to every session at once, stays after restarts (`disabledExtensions` and `enabledExtensions` in `~/.pi-pocket/config.json`), and everyone in a session sees who changed what. Turning Lancet Guard off here leaves Pi's own setting (`~/.pi/lancet-guard.json`) alone. Under PiP, the owner picks when PiP compacts its context. The system prompt cannot be turned off.
- **Uploads.** Attach files (or paste images). Files are saved on the server and their paths are given to the agent; images also go straight to models that accept them.
- **Rich replies.** Code blocks in Pi's replies show their language and a Copy button, with syntax colors for the common languages (no dependencies, so it works offline), and a block of more than 40 lines folds until you ask for the rest. Some show more than code, with the code a tab away: an `html` block (or a reply that is a whole page) is previewed as a page, inline, with its scripts off and nothing loaded from anywhere; **▶ Run** runs its scripts in the artifacts' sandbox, where it can reach the internet but not the app; an `svg` block is drawn as an image (tap it for full screen); and a `diff` or `patch` block opens in the diff viewer. While Pi is still writing, they show as code. Write cards color the file they write, and the file viewer previews `.html` files the same way, with Run.
- **Images inline.** Attached images show as thumbnails (also in the composer before you send). When Pi writes `![description](path)` with a path on the server (absolute, `~/…`, or relative to the session's folder), the image shows in its reply. Images a tool returns and SVG artifacts show inline too. Tap any image to see it full screen. Only real image files are shown: the server checks a file's bytes, not just its name.
- **Launcher.** `npm start` asks how your devices should reach Pi Pocket: this device only, the local network, a Cloudflare Tunnel, or Tailscale. It then shows the address and a QR code to sign in your phone, and keeps running with keys to restart the server or change access.
